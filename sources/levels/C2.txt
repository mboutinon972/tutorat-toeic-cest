TEXT Éditorial : le coût discret de la tarification dynamique
The Quiet Cost of Dynamic Pricing
When a ride-hailing app raises its fares during a storm, economists tend to nod: higher prices draw more drivers onto the road, they argue, and allocate scarce rides to those who value them most. Yet the same logic, applied more broadly, is producing consequences that its proponents rarely anticipated. As retailers adopt algorithms that adjust prices minute by minute, shoppers have begun to suspect that they are being charged according to what a system infers they will tolerate, rather than what a product costs. A study by researchers at the University of Aldersgate found that, when told that prices varied by customer, nearly two-thirds of respondents said they would shop less often at the retailer in question, even if their own prices were lower than average. The finding suggests that the damage lies less in the price itself than in the perception of unfairness. Companies that adopt dynamic pricing without transparency may therefore be trading short-term margins for long-term trust, a bargain that is difficult to reverse once consumers' suspicions are aroused. Some firms are responding by publishing the principles behind their pricing, and by capping the speed and size of changes. Whether such measures will reassure the public, or merely draw attention to a practice many would prefer to ignore, remains to be seen.
